The Origin

A bad season built something better.

Last maple season, the Princess was projecting double the previous year's output. The trees were tapped. The equipment was staged. The fire was ready.

20

Twenty bottles came out. The season was a total flop — and there was no obvious reason why.

So she pulled three years of weather data and analyzed every variable that drives a maple tree. Freeze-thaw cycles. Diurnal swing. Soil temperature. Solar radiation. Snow depth.

The answer came back clear: it wasn't the methodology. It was the weather. The season failed — not the operator.

That analysis became the Maple Sap Predictor. The data-driven way to know when to tap, when to collect, and when to wait.

The Science of Freeze & Flow

01

Freeze-Thaw Physics

Sap flows when daytime temps rise above 0°C and nights drop below freezing. These cycles create pressure within the sapwood that drives exudation.

02

Live Weather Data

Pulling hourly temperature, soil conditions, solar radiation, snow depth, and vapor pressure deficit from Environment Canada forecast models.

03

Machine Learning

An XGBoost model trained on 3 years of Sudbury-area data scores each day from 0–100. Prime, Good, Low, or No Flow — before it happens.

04

Your Forecast

Wake up to a daily score. Plan your collection runs. Stop guessing. Whether you have 5 taps or 500, timing is everything.
